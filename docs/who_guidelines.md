# World Health Organization (WHO) Clinical Triage Guidelines

## 1. Acute Upper Respiratory Infections & Seasonal Influenza
Acute respiratory tract infections are classified based on anatomical location and severity of symptoms:
- **Mild URI**: Mild fever (<38.5°C), rhinorrhea, sore throat, cough without dyspnea. Recommended management includes oral fluid rehydration, rest, and temperature monitoring.
- **Severe Acute Respiratory Infection (SARI)**: Fever >=38.5°C accompanied by shortness of breath, tachypnea (respiratory rate >30 breaths/min in adults), or arterial oxygen saturation (SpO2) <92%. Immediate triage to emergency facility required.

## 2. Pediatric Febrile Illness Protocol
- **Infants <3 Months**: Any fever >=38.0°C (100.4°F) is a medical emergency requiring urgent septic workup and lumbar puncture evaluation.
- **Children 3-36 Months**: Fever >=39.0°C (102.2°F) with lethargy, poor capillary refill (>2 seconds), stiff neck, or petechial rash signals bacterial meningitis or sepsis. Immediate pediatric ER transfer mandatory.

## 3. Acute Diarrheal Disease & Dehydration Triage
- **No Dehydration**: Alert, normal skin turgor, drinks normally. Administer Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) 75 mL/kg.
- **Moderate Dehydration**: Restless, sunken eyes, skin pinch goes back slowly (<2 sec). Administer ORS under clinical supervision.
- **Severe Dehydration**: Lethargic/unconscious, skin pinch goes back very slowly (>2 sec), weak radial pulse. Requires urgent IV Ringer's Lactate or Normal Saline resuscitation.
